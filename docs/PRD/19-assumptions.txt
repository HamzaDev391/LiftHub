19. Assumptions

This section documents assumptions that influence product decisions but are not themselves functional requirements. If an assumption later proves false, the product, architecture, or implementation may need to change.

ID	Assumption	Design Impact

A-01	Every Member owns their own workout data; no workout data is shared between users in V1.	Supports simple Row-Level Security (RLS) policies and single-user ownership across all workout-related tables.

A-02	A Member generally trains using structured resistance-training workouts consisting of exercises, sets, reps, and weight.	Allows Workout Sessions, Statistics, and PR calculations to remain optimized for resistance training.

A-03	Members usually carry their mobile phone during workouts.	Justifies the mobile-first experience and eliminates the need for companion devices in V1.

A-04	Gym internet connectivity cannot be relied upon.	Reinforces offline-first behavior defined in FR-SESS-11, NFR-REL-02, P4, and P5.

A-05	Each Member has exactly one active profile linked to one authenticated account.	Simplifies ownership relationships between profiles, routines, sessions, and statistics.

A-06	Built-in exercises cover the majority of common gym exercises, while custom exercises handle gym-specific variations.	Supports the recommendation made in FR-EXLIB-04 without requiring a community-maintained exercise database.

A-07	Workout data will continue growing over multiple years.	Requires scalable database indexing, pagination, and efficient historical queries.

A-08	The application is intended primarily for individual use, not collaborative training.	Allows multi-user collaboration to remain outside V1 scope.

A-09	The majority of Members log sets immediately after completing them rather than after finishing the workout.	Supports the "During Workout Principle" established in Section 2.

A-10	Every authenticated Member is trusted only with their own data. No administrative interface exists in V1.	Simplifies authorization rules and removes the need for elevated application roles.

Design Impact:
These assumptions directly influence future ERD decisions, particularly ownership relationships, indexing strategy, synchronization design, and Row-Level Security policies. Any assumption invalidated in future versions should trigger an architecture review.
